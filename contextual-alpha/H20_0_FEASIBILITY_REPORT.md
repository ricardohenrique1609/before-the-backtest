# H20.0 — Context Data Feasibility: Report

**Verdict: PARTIAL PASS.** Three of the five context layers (C1 macro, C2 political/regulatory,
C3 crypto-systemic) pass every pre-registered criterion but are **CONDITIONAL** on measured
temporal biases. The two layers closest to the venue and the asset (C4 exchange-specific, C5
asset-specific) **fail**: their documents cannot be shown to read today as they read at `t`, and
assets cannot be named point-in-time often enough.

This is a statement about the **data substrate only**. It says nothing about whether context
predicts anything. C4 and C5 are **UNTESTED / DATA-GATE FAIL**, not economically rejected.

## 4. Coverage

- Every probed source was available at **100%** of sample timestamps except: GDELT (2 of 70 slice
  files missing — the English and translingual files of the same slice, 2018-06-19 13:45 → 34/35
  timestamps), and **CFTC (2018–2020 only; 2021–2026 blocked by
  HTTP 429 and recorded as MISSING, not zero)**.
- Mean documents per 7-day window (`evidence/H20_0_COVERAGE_BY_SOURCE_YEAR.csv`): Binance 5.5 (2018)
  → 30 (2026); SEC 7.5 → 3.7; Fed 7.8 → 1.0; Federal Register crypto-term hits 0.2 → 5.7; GDELT
  ~2,700–5,600 records per 15-minute slice, of which **0.28%** are crypto-tagged (399 in 35 slices).
- **Asset-specific coverage is thin.** Only **17.1%** of the 105 asset-time units have any
  asset-specific document in the 30 days before `t` (any source); **7.6%** have one that is not a
  Binance notice. Missing context is not zero context: for 40 of 105 units no PIT name exists, so
  name-based sources are *not searchable* for them at all (`evidence/H20_0_C5_ASSET_COVERAGE.csv`).

## 5. PIT timestamp quality

| class | share (all sources ex-GDELT, by document) |
|---|---|
| T0_EXACT | 66% (2018) → 84% (2026) |
| T1_MINUTE_OR_HOUR | Fed feed (time in US Eastern by site convention) |
| T2_DAY_ONLY | Federal Register, ALFRED vintages (day-end rule applied) |
| T3 / T4 | **0%** of admitted documents |

Every collected document has a usable availability time; none needed T3/T4. Two cautions:
SEC/CFTC stamps are **CMS publish stamps** not printed on the page; checked against first archive
capture, **0** SEC and **0** CFTC stamps were contradicted (no capture earlier than the stamp). One
Fed release (2022) has a first capture from **1998** — the Fed reused the URL path, so the capture is a
different document; it is flagged `url_reuse_suspect` and does not count as archived (deviation D7).
Binance's `releaseDate` was likewise never contradicted (0 of 104). GDELT is a first-observed clock:
for about half of 2021–2026 articles an archive capture predates GDELT's slice, as expected — the
article was public earlier than GDELT saw it, so GDELT time is a conservative bound.

**T4 measured, not assumed.** On Wikipedia's Current-Events pages, 13.5% of the items present today
were absent at `t` (4.8% for days already complete at `t`), and **96.4%** of the pages were edited
after `t` (median 6 edits). Reading today's version of a past day page would leak; reading the
revision current at `t` does not.

## 6. Version / mutability quality

| source | basis | content as of `t` reconstructible (V0/V1/V2) |
|---|---|---|
| Federal Register, ALFRED, GDELT records | immutable by construction | 100% |
| Wikipedia portal / asset pages | full revision history | 100% |
| SEC | archive capture ≤ `t`, n = 99 | 87.9% |
| Fed | archive capture ≤ `t`, n = 75 | 76.0% (53% in 2018–20, 95% in 2024–26; 1 capture excluded as URL reuse) |
| CFTC | archive capture ≤ `t`, n = 17 (2018 only) | 23.5% |
| **Binance CMS** | archive capture ≤ `t`, n = 104 | **38.5%** (2.9% in 2018–20, 86% in 2021–23, 24% in 2024–26) |

Binance exposes `version` (0/1 only — not an edit counter) and `lastUpdateTime` (non-zero for 4.2%
of window articles, which are classed V4: known to be edited, original lost). For the rest only the
current body survives; absence of edit evidence is not evidence of no edit (V3, not credited).

Two URL-identity traps were measured: **SEC moved its press releases** from `/news/press-release/`
to `/newsroom/press-releases/` in 2024 — the current URL is captured ≤ `t` for 20% of releases, the
URL in force at the time for 68%. **Binance URLs gained a title slug** around 2023; a code-only
lookup undercounted captures (deviation D6, corrected).

## 7. Entity-resolution quality

- **Exchange-level (C4):** of 161 parenthesised tickers in window notices, 79.5% resolve to an asset
  era that existed at publication or to a new entity the notice itself creates. The misses are mostly
  instruments outside the spot universe (tokenised stocks GOOGL/PYPL/QCOM, fiat BND, the network
  label BEP20, the BVOL index, Binance Alpha tokens) plus 6 references to an asset before its spot
  listing. The criterion was fixed in code before inspection and is **not** redefined after the
  fact to clear the bar by 0.5 pp.
- **Asset names (C5):** names were taken only from Binance listing notices published ≤ `t`. Parser v2
  on the untouched validation draw resolves **58.1%** (v1: 46.7%); 36.2% fail because the only
  listing notice carries a bare ticker ("Binance Lists ADA", the 2017–2018 house style), 5.7% have no
  listing notice at all (assets listed before the catalogue). Resolution rises from **44%** (2018–20)
  to **73%** (2024–26). Of the 61 names resolved on the validation draw, all point to the right asset;
  58 are exact, 2 are pre-rebrand names (ZenCash for Horizen, SuperFarm for SuperVerse) and 1 carries
  title debris.
- **Ticker eras:** no sampled ticker had a later re-use (conflict rate 0%), so no as-of-`t` mapping
  differed from a today-mapping in this sample. 3 of 105 units are Binance leveraged tokens (BLVT),
  venue products rather than projects.
- **Wikipedia as a C5 source:** of 65 PIT-named units, 3 have a crypto article as of `t`; 8 hit a
  disambiguation page, 15 a non-crypto article, 37 nothing, 2 an article created after `t`.

## 8. Reproducibility

Refetch hash stability: Federal Register, Fed, SEC, Wikipedia (`oldid` sha1), GDELT slices and
ALFRED vintages **100%**; Binance 58/58 identical, 2/60 refetches refused by a transient block
(96.7% success). Request outcomes per source: `evidence/H20_0_REQUEST_OUTCOMES.csv`. One request-log
line in 4,490 was corrupted by concurrent writers (deviation D3).

## 9. Survivorship and missingness risks

- **Source survivorship (sealed stage, `evidence/H20_0_SEALED_SURVIVORSHIP.json`).** Run after the
  pre-seal hash manifest was verified; 54 units belong to assets no longer listed, 51 to listed ones.
  Asset-specific coverage 18.5% vs 15.7%, name resolution 57% vs 67%, PIT Wikipedia article 1.9% vs
  3.9%. **No severe gap.** At this sample size this does not show survivorship is absent, only that
  it is not large.
- **Entity popularity — severe.** Any asset-specific document in 30 days: **5.7% (low-liquidity
  tercile) vs 31.4% (high)**, a 5.5× ratio. Non-venue documents: 5.7% vs 11.4%.
- **Archive bias — severe** for Binance, Fed and GDELT article URLs: what can be shown to have read
  the same at `t` depends strongly on the era (GDELT article captured ≤ `t`: 5% in 2018–20 vs 51%
  in 2024–26).
- **Temporal coverage:** coverage shares are flat across years for C1–C4 (no severe gap); document
  counts are not (Binance ×2.9 from 2018–20 to 2024–26) — a venue publishing more, measured, not
  corrected.
- **Language — severe.** 64% of GDELT records come from the translingual feed, but the crypto tag
  fires at 0.14% there vs 0.54% in English: an English keyword list under-detects non-English crypto
  news by roughly 4×. C1–C2 sources are US-only; Binance was harvested in English only.
- **Missingness ≠ zero** is kept throughout (`evidence/H20_0_SOURCE_TIMESTAMP_STATUS.csv`,
  `evidence/H20_0_MISSINGNESS.csv`).

## 10. Licensing constraints

US federal sources are public domain. Wikipedia is CC BY-SA 4.0 (share-alike). GDELT metadata is
open with citation; the articles it points to are not. Binance notices are proprietary: derived
features for research are workable, **redistribution of text is not**. ALFRED/FRED and Wayback
captures carry use terms that rule out bulk republication. Evidence files hold ids, URLs and
aggregates only; raw content stays under `data/` (gitignored).

## 11. Estimated scalability

| source | per-`t` cost observed | full weekly panel 2018–2026 (~460 `t`) |
|---|---|---|
| Binance titles | one harvest (~215 requests, ~7 min) covers all `t` | trivial |
| Binance bodies | ~23 per `t` at 1.9 s | ~10.5k bodies, ~6 h |
| Federal Register / ALFRED / Fed | 1–3 requests per `t` | minutes |
| SEC | 1–2 month listings per `t` | ~110 listings |
| CFTC | throttled (429) | **blocked** without a slower, multi-day schedule |
| GDELT GKG | 2 files (~17 MB) per 15-min slice | one slice per `t` is cheap; a full 7-day window is 672 slices per `t` (~11 GB) — needs a narrower filter or BigQuery |
| Wayback | ~1.2 s per lookup, intermittent outages | the binding constraint for V1 checks |

## 12. Blockers

1. **C4 version quality.** Binance notice bodies exist only in their current form; 38.5% can be
   shown to read the same at `t`, and almost none from 2018–2020. Notice **titles and timestamps**
   are excellent (T0, complete catalogue); notice **text** is not PIT-reconstructible.
2. **C5 naming.** 42% of asset-time units have no point-in-time name, so no third-party source can be
   searched for them without future knowledge; the gap is worst in the early years.
3. **C5 density and popularity bias.** Even where searchable, asset-specific context is sparse and
   concentrated in liquid assets.
4. **CFTC throttling** (2021–2026 missing) and **US-only** C1/C2.
5. **Language:** English-only keyword detection misses most non-English crypto coverage.

## 13. H20.0 gate verdict

**PARTIAL PASS** (`H20_0_GATE.json`).

- C1, C2, C3: all criteria pass; **CONDITIONAL** on the biases in §9.
- C4: **DATA-GATE FAIL / UNTESTED** — G4 (38.5% < 70%), G7 (79.5% < 80%).
- C5: **DATA-GATE FAIL / UNTESTED** — G4 (39.8%), G7 (58.1% < 80%), severe popularity bias.

## 14. Recommendation for H20.1 (not started)

H20.1 should not be opened on all five layers. If the sponsor authorises it, the defensible scope is:

1. **C1–C3 only, as metadata and structured series**: ALFRED vintages, Federal Register / SEC / Fed
   event flags and counts, GDELT GKG theme/count features, Wikipedia revision-as-of-`t` items. Each
   CONDITIONAL caveat must be carried as a design constraint (era-stratified evaluation; no claim
   about non-English or non-US context).
2. **Binance notice titles and timestamps as an R1/R2 (flags and counts) source** — they are T0 and
   complete — explicitly **without** body text. This is a narrower C4 than planned and would need its
   own pre-registered feasibility check, because the gate failed on version quality of text, not of
   titles.
3. **C5 stays closed** until a PIT naming source is found. Two candidates worth one probe each:
   parsing names from the *bodies* of the 2017–2018 "Binance Lists TICKER" notices (bodies are
   current-version, so only the name extraction, not the content, would be used), and the archived
   versions of those notices.
4. Before any of this, the sponsor should decide whether the truncated H18.1b catalogue snapshot
   (§1) needs an assessment against the frozen H18/H19 results.

**STOP.** H20.1 is not begun.
